I think the most practical solution for Cruzia RP would be a flexible company disclosure system, where players explain what their businesses are doing without being required to produce financial reports, technical documentation, or detailed project timelines.

The goal should be to communicate meaningful company activity, not simulate corporate paperwork.

## 1. Introduce a simple disclosure principle

A company disclosure should ideally answer three questions:

1. What did the company do?
2. How did it do it?
3. Why did it do it?

That's enough for most ordinary corporate developments.

Players shouldn't need exact budgets, employee counts, production volumes, or elaborate explanations unless those details are important to the action being taken.

## 2. Allow different levels of detail

I'd recommend three informal levels of disclosure rather than mandatory reporting formats.

Level 1

Brief update — Routine company activity

One or two sentences. Suitable for ordinary operations, minor improvements, and continued development.

Throughout 2001, Newbridge Construction continued undertaking commercial and industrial construction projects, with particular emphasis on rehabilitating aging manufacturing facilities in Piranburgh.

Level 2

Development summary — Meaningful progress

A short paragraph explaining what changed, the general approach, and its purpose.

During 2001, Strata Engineering & Feasibility expanded its industrial consultancy activities, placing greater emphasis on brownfield assessments, structural evaluations, and infrastructure planning. The company also invested in improved surveying equipment and specialist training to support increasingly complex engineering assignments.

Level 3

Major announcement — Significant developments

More detail for major investments, new factories, substantial acquisitions, important products, or developments involving other players or government authorities.

These can use press releases, project announcements, detailed summaries, or structured proposals, depending on what makes sense.

These levels shouldn't impose mandatory word counts. They simply help players judge how much explanation an activity deserves.

## 3. Let players summarize entire periods

This is probably the most important improvement.

Players should be allowed to cover several months or even an entire year in one disclosure.

For example:

EXAMPLE — 2001 COMPANY ACTIVITY SUMMARY

Newbridge Construction Ltd.

Throughout 2001, Newbridge continued its commercial and industrial construction activities, with much of its work concentrated on rehabilitating and modernizing existing facilities. The company also pursued additional warehouse and logistics construction opportunities in New Cruzia City and Piranburgh.

During the year, Newbridge improved its construction equipment and project-management capabilities to accommodate increasingly demanding industrial assignments.

The company's priorities remained focused on industrial construction, commercial development, and infrastructure works.

That is a perfectly reasonable disclosure for an entire year of routine activity.

Not every company needs an exciting development every month, and players shouldn't be expected to manufacture events merely to demonstrate that their business is still operating.

## 4. Distinguish company actions from outcomes

This is where I'd introduce a small but important rule.

Players should generally be free to describe reasonable actions their companies can undertake themselves, but significant external outcomes should remain subject to the appropriate RP rules or GM adjudication.

| Company-controlled activity                                         | May require adjudication                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Improving internal procedures                                       | Winning a major government contract                              |
| Training employees                                                  | Achieving market dominance                                       |
| Undertaking routine private-sector work within established capacity | Receiving a massive foreign investment                           |
| Beginning product research                                          | Successfully developing revolutionary technology                 |
| Conducting an internal feasibility study                            | Obtaining permits or approval for a major infrastructure project |
| Pursuing commercial customers                                       | Securing a major deal with another player’s company              |

The distinction is between what the company decides or does and what external parties accept or what results are achieved.

For example, a player can reasonably announce that their construction company is pursuing government infrastructure contracts. They shouldn't automatically declare that the government awarded them Ƶ2 billion worth of projects.

## 5. Don't require unnecessary figures

Players may have no realistic way to calculate their company's exact finances, production capacity, staffing, or operating costs.

They should be allowed to use qualitative descriptions such as:

- The company gradually expanded its engineering workforce.
- Production efficiency improved following equipment upgrades.
- Demand for the company's services remained stable.
- The company began exploring opportunities in overseas markets.

These communicate useful developments without inventing numbers.

However, qualitative language shouldn't become a loophole for unearned results. Saying a company improved its production processes is different from claiming it doubled its national market share.

## 6. Suggested rule for Cruzia RP

This is how I'd phrase it for players:

### Company Activity & Disclosure Guidelines

Players are encouraged to disclose meaningful developments involving their companies, but are not required to produce detailed corporate reports, financial statements, or technical documentation for ordinary business activities.

**Disclosure format and length are flexible.** Players may use brief updates, development summaries, announcements, or more detailed reports according to the significance of the activity.

A disclosure should generally explain:

- **What** the company is doing or has done.
- **How** the activity is being undertaken, where relevant.
- **Why** the company is pursuing it.

Players may consolidate routine activities into monthly, quarterly, or annual summaries. Uneventful periods may be skipped entirely.

Exact financial figures, workforce numbers, production statistics, and technical specifications are optional unless required by applicable RP rules or necessary to assess a significant action.

Companies may reasonably describe their own internal decisions, routine operations, and incremental progress within established capabilities. Major external outcomes, exceptional growth, disputed achievements, government decisions, and interactions affecting other players remain subject to the relevant approval or adjudication process.

**The purpose of disclosure is to establish clear, understandable company progression, not to reward document length or administrative complexity.**

## 7. One additional recommendation: Separate disclosures from applications

I would distinguish between ordinary company lore and actions that actually require a GM decision.

A disclosure tells the RP what a company has done within its established authority. An application or proposal asks for approval or adjudication of something beyond that authority.

That way, a player who simply wants to describe a year's worth of construction work doesn't have to submit a formal proposal, while someone attempting to build a nuclear power plant, establish a major new industry, or obtain an enormous state contract still goes through the necessary process.

The core philosophy should be that players are judged by the plausibility and significance of their actions, not by how professionally they can write corporate documentation.

This would make business RP considerably more approachable for casual players without removing the depth available to those who enjoy detailed corporate simulation.
